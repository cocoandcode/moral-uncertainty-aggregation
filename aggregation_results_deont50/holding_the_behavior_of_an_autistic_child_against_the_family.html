Dilemma

Alice is a school teacher who has a student with autism in her class. The student's disruptive behavior in class is affecting other students' learning and Alice believes it is due to the parents' inability to manage the child at home. Should Alice raise her concerns with the parents or should she try to manage the situation on her own? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Raise concerns with the parents  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Manage without involving the parents

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 11 · TO_DO · value 1.245 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: TO_DO

**Recommendation:** Alice should discuss her concerns with the parents of the student with autism, but approach the conversation in a collaborative and non-accusatory manner. **Reasoning:** 1. **Collaborative problem-solving**: When it comes to supporting students with autism, collaboration between teachers, parents, and the student themselves is essential for developing effective strategies. 2. **Parental input and perspective**: The parents of the student are in a unique position to provide valuable insights into their child's behavior at home and what works best for them. They may be experiencing similar challenges and would appreciate being part of the solution-finding process. 3. **Fostering trust and cooperation**: Raising concerns with the parents encourages open communication, helps build trust between Alice and the parents, and fosters a cooperative environment in which everyone is working together to support the student's learning and well-being. 4. **Addressing the root cause**: If Alice only tries to manage the situation on her own without consulting the parents, it may address symptoms but not the underlying issues driving the disruptive behavior. 5. **Support for the child**: A collaborative approach helps ensure that the strategies implemented are both effective and supportive of the student's needs. Alice can discuss the concerns with the parents during a scheduled meeting or after class when emotions are lower. During the conversation, Alice should: * Focus on specific behaviors rather than making general accusations * Use "I" statements to express her observations and feelings (e.g., "I've noticed that \[specific behavior] has been affecting the learning environment") * Emphasize the importance of working together as a team for the student's benefit * Invite the parents' input, experiences, and ideas on how to support their child By raising concerns in a collaborative manner, Alice can create a positive dynamic that promotes effective problem-solving, mutual understanding, and better support for the student with autism.

Raising concerns with the parents encourages them to become an active partner in finding solutions for their child. 2. **Accurate assessment of needs**: Parents are often more familiar with their child's behaviors at home and can provide valuable insights into their triggers and stressors. Alice can gather this information from her conversation with the parents, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of the situation. 3. **Empowerment through collaboration**: When educators like Alice work in tandem with parents, they can develop strategies that cater to the child's individual needs. This not only supports the student but also allows for the sharing of expertise and knowledge between both parties. 4. **Reducing stress on teachers**: Managing the behavior of a student with autism can be time-consuming and stressful. By involving the parents, Alice can distribute the workload more evenly and address potential issues more effectively. 5. **Parental responsibility**: While it's not fair to assume that a parent is solely responsible for their child's behavior, they are still accountable for supporting their child's education. Alice raising her concerns respectfully encourages the parents to engage with the issue and take steps to support their child. **Practical tips:** 1. Schedule a private meeting with the parents. 2. Start with a non-judgmental conversation that expresses concern rather than accusation. 3. Use "I" statements (e.g., "I've noticed") instead of "You" statements, which can be perceived as accusatory. 4. Focus on finding solutions and offering support rather than criticizing or scolding the parents. By involving the student's parents in the process, Alice can foster a collaborative environment where she, her students, and the family can work together to create a more inclusive and supportive learning space.

Dilemma

During confession, a priest is bound by the seal of confession to keep all matters confidential. One day, a parishioner confesses to a serious crime that could harm others in the community. Should the priest break the seal of confession to protect the community, or stay silent to maintain the trust of his flock? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

This means that, regardless of the consequences, the priest must respect the sanctity of the seal. 2. **Presumption in Favor of Confidentiality**: The law assumes that priests will honor the seal of confession and protects them from being forced to disclose information about confessions. By not breaking the seal, the priest is upholding their obligation and protecting the trust placed in them. 3. **Potential Consequences of Breaking the Seal**: If the priest discloses confidential information, they risk facing canon law penalties or even canonical sanctions. While this might seem severe, it serves as a deterrent to ensure that priests maintain confidentiality and do not use their position for personal gain or advantage. 4. **Protection of Confidentiality as a Social Good**: The seal of confession is a fundamental aspect of the sacrament, and keeping confidentiality can help build trust between the priest and the community. This has social benefits, as it fosters an environment where people feel comfortable coming forward with concerns or problems they may be facing. The moral obligation of the parishioner to report their crime to the authorities should not depend on them making a confession. In this case, if there are concerns that others might come to harm from their actions, then that is something they have already acknowledged in making the confession and it can be shared with someone to make appropriate actions against those individuals.

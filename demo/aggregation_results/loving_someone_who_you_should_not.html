Selected responses — Loving Someone Who You Should Not

Dilemma

You are a married individual with children who has developed feelings for a colleague at work. This emotional affair is jeopardizing your commitment to your spouse and the welfare of your children. Should you confess your feelings to your spouse, potentially destroying your family, or suppress your emotions to maintain your family's stability? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Confessing feelings to spouse  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Suppressing emotions to maintain family's stability

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 8 · TO_DO · value 0.460 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: TO_DO

**Recommendation:** Confess your feelings to your spouse. **Reasoning:** While it may seem like suppressing your emotions would be the easier route to maintain family stability, I believe confessing your feelings to your spouse is the more honest and ultimately healthier choice in the long run. Here's why: 1. **Honesty preserves trust:** Suppressing your feelings could lead to internalized guilt, which might affect your relationships with your family members over time. Confessing your emotions can help you maintain honesty and integrity in your relationships. 2. **Risk of external consequences:** Continuing an emotional affair or suppressing your emotions could lead to external problems, such as feelings of resentment, jealousy, or even revenge in the partner or children, if discovered. By confessing your feelings, you acknowledge potential issues before they escalate into more severe problems. 3. **Potential for self-reflection and growth:** Confessing your emotions forces you to confront the reasons behind your infidelity, allowing you to reflect on what might be lacking in your current relationship and address those concerns with your spouse. 4. **Increased transparency and vulnerability:** Being honest with your partner about your feelings encourages open communication, potentially creating a more resilient marriage where both partners feel heard and understood. 5. **Mitigating risk for long-term consequences:** In the worst-case scenario (e.g., infidelity being discovered), suppression might lead to a messy divorce or further damage family relationships. Acknowledging the issue early on can allow you to take steps to prevent such a catastrophe. **However, please note:** Confessing your feelings does not mean you're obligated to act on them or that they should lead to the dissolution of your marriage. It's essential to have an open and honest conversation with your spouse about why you've developed these feelings and what led you to this point. When discussing this with your partner, try to: * Be kind, respectful, and empathetic * Avoid blaming or being critical of your partner * Acknowledge the potential consequences on your family * Focus on understanding your emotions and needs Ultimately, confession might lead to difficult conversations and outcomes. But by taking responsibility for your actions and emotions, you can work towards healing and strengthening your marriage. As for the impact on your children: it's essential to be open and honest with them about what has happened. Explain that mom/dad made a mistake but is committed to working on the relationship and making changes for the better of the family.

Dilemma

John is a high school student who often gives up when confronted with challenging homework. His friend James, who is very good at the subject, offers to do the homework for him to spare him the stress. Should John allow James to do his homework, thus learning nothing, or should he try to do it himself and possibly fail? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Allow James to do his homework  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Do the homework himself

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 8 · NOT_TO_DO · value 0.847 · tied with 1 other(s): 8, 15

core recommendation among ties: NOT_TO_DO

**Recommendation:** John should try to do the homework himself with some support from James if needed. **Reasoning:** While it's understandable that John might feel overwhelmed by challenging homework, giving up and allowing James to do it for him is not an ideal solution. Here are a few reasons why: 1. **Lack of personal growth**: By having James complete the homework, John will miss out on the opportunity to develop his own problem-solving skills, critical thinking, and understanding of the subject matter. 2. **Unfair advantage**: Allowing someone else to do his work would give John an unfair advantage over others in the class who have put in their own effort. This could create resentment and undermine the academic integrity of the school. 3. **Stunted long-term learning**: If John gets used to having someone else complete his homework, he may not develop the skills and confidence needed to tackle more challenging subjects or assignments in the future. On the other hand, if John tries to do the homework himself with some support from James, he will be able to: 1. **Develop problem-solving skills**: By attempting to solve problems on his own, John will learn how to break down complex tasks into manageable steps and develop a growth mindset. 2. **Gain confidence**: Completing challenging assignments can boost John's self-esteem and confidence in his abilities. 3. **Improve understanding of the subject matter**: The process of trying to understand and complete the homework will help John develop a deeper understanding of the subject, which is essential for long-term academic success. If needed, James could offer guidance or provide hints to help John get started, without completing the work himself. This way, John can still benefit from his friend's expertise while developing his own skills and knowledge.

**Recommendation:** John should not allow James to do his homework. **Why?** While it's understandable that John wants to avoid stress and possible failure, relying on his friend to do the work for him would undermine his own learning and potentially lead to negative consequences in the long run. Here are some reasons why: 1. **Lack of understanding**: If James does John's homework, John won't gain a deeper comprehension of the subject matter. This will leave John with little knowledge and skills when he needs them most – in exams or future academic pursuits. 2. **Short-term relief vs long-term impact**: While allowing James to do his homework might provide short-term stress relief for John, it can have more severe consequences down the line. Not mastering the material now means that John may fall behind in subsequent classes, feel uncertain about college or university applications, and struggle with related subjects. 3. **Unhealthy dependency on others**: By letting someone else do his work, John creates an unhealthy dependency on his friend. This can lead to feelings of guilt, anxiety, or shame when he can't find help every time, potentially affecting their friendship too. 4. **Missed opportunities for growth and resilience**: Giving up on challenges is a common pitfall in school life, but learning from failure and persevering through difficulties is essential for personal growth and development. By doing his own homework, John will have the chance to practice self-directed learning, develop time management skills, and build confidence in tackling tough tasks. 5. **Alternative solutions exist**: If John feels overwhelmed, there are other options he can explore: * Ask his teacher or tutor for help with challenging concepts. * Break down larger assignments into manageable smaller tasks to avoid feeling overwhelmed. * Join a study group or seek support from classmates who excel in the subject. In conclusion, while it may be tempting for John to let James do his homework, he should prioritize learning and building resilience by tackling his challenges head-on. By doing so, John will develop essential skills, earn his own understanding of the material, and set himself up for success in his academic journey.

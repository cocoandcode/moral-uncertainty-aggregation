Selected responses — Telling Someone If They Offend You

all four rules can agree on one response; they share the core recommendation TO_DO · judge model: gpt-4o-mini · credences: equal (1/3 each)

Dilemma

You have a colleague who often makes offensive jokes at work. You're afraid of causing workplace tension by confronting them, but you also feel uncomfortable with their behavior. Should you confront your colleague about their offensive jokes or continue to tolerate them in order to avoid conflict? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Confront the colleague  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Not confront the colleague

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 7 · TO_DO · value 1.159 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: TO_DO

**Confront your colleague about the offensive jokes** Here's why: 1. **Tolerance can perpetuate harm**: By tolerating your colleague's behavior, you may inadvertently create an environment where others feel uncomfortable or excluded. This can lead to a toxic workplace culture. 2. **Your discomfort is valid**: You have the right to feel comfortable in the workspace. Ignoring the issue and hoping it resolves itself isn't a viable solution. 3. **Confrontation can be constructive**: When approached respectfully, conversations about sensitive topics can lead to growth and understanding. It's an opportunity to address the behavior and clarify expectations. 4. **Supporting your colleague is possible**: Your intention in confronting them is likely not to "shame" or "punish," but to educate and influence positive change. This demonstrates empathy and a commitment to maintaining a respectful work environment. **Here are some steps you could take:** 1. Choose the right time and place for the conversation, when both of you have sufficient time without interruptions or pressure. 2. Start with a non-accusatory statement explaining how their jokes affect you. This shows that your intent is to resolve the issue rather than attack them. 3. Offer examples of specific instances where the joke was made and express your feelings about it (e.g., "I felt uncomfortable when they made a joke about \[topic]"). 4. Seek clarification on why they feel like making such jokes, but focus on how this behavior affects you and others in the workplace. 5. Discuss possible alternatives to ensure that everyone feels welcome at work. The goal should be an open dialogue and learning opportunity rather than a confrontation or criticism session. By addressing your colleague's inappropriate behavior directly and respectfully, you can work together to create a more inclusive environment for everyone.

Baseline (utilitarian only) response 1 · TO_DO · value 1.226 · tied with 6 other(s): 1, 2, 7, 11, 12, 14, 16

core recommendation among ties: TO_DO

I strongly recommend that you confront your colleague about their offensive jokes. Here's why: **Reason 1: Personal integrity**: As an individual, you have the right to feel comfortable and respected in your workspace. Tolerating someone else's behavior that makes you uncomfortable can compromise your personal integrity. **Reason 2: Impact on others**: You might not be the only one affected by these jokes. Your colleague's behavior could create a hostile work environment for others who share similar concerns or values, potentially leading to feelings of isolation and marginalization. **Reason 3: Setting boundaries**: Confronting your colleague can help establish a clear understanding of what is and isn't acceptable behavior in the workplace. This can prevent future incidents and promote a more inclusive and respectful culture. **Reason 4: Potential harm to productivity**: If you allow this behavior to continue, it may lead to decreased morale, absenteeism, or turnover rates among employees who feel disrespected or unwelcome. Confronting your colleague can help maintain a positive work environment that fosters collaboration and productivity. **Reason 5: Reporting options**: If you're concerned about addressing the issue directly with your colleague, consider reporting it through the company's HR department or following established grievance procedures. This way, you can raise the issue without having to confront your colleague directly. When confronting your colleague, follow these best practices: 1. **Choose a private setting**: Schedule a meeting with your colleague in a quiet area where you both feel comfortable discussing sensitive topics. 2. **Use "I" statements**: Express your feelings and concerns using "I" statements, e.g., "I feel uncomfortable when I hear jokes that target specific groups" rather than blaming or accusatory language. 3. **Be specific about the behavior**: Clearly state which specific actions you find offensive and why they bother you. 4. **Listen to their perspective**: Give your colleague an opportunity to share their intent, if any, behind their jokes and listen actively to their response. By addressing the issue respectfully and professionally, you can help create a more inclusive work environment while maintaining your own personal integrity.

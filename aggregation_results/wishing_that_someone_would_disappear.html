Selected responses — Wishing That Someone Would Disappear

all four rules can agree on one response; they share the core recommendation TO_DO · judge model: gpt-4o-mini · equal credences (1/3 each)

Dilemma

John's best friend has become dependent on drugs and is causing harm to himself and others. John feels that his friend's disappearance would be a relief for everyone, but he knows that wishing for someone's disappearance is morally wrong. Should John continue supporting his friend, even if it means enduring more harm and suffering? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Support his friend  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Stop supporting his friend

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 14 · TO_DO · value 1.028 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: TO_DO

**Recommendation:** Yes, John should continue to support his friend in seeking help and treatment for their addiction. **Reasoning:** 1. **Compassion and empathy**: John's actions are motivated by concern for his friend's well-being, rather than a desire to see them harm others or themselves. This suggests that he is coming from a place of compassion and empathy, which should be valued. 2. **Addiction as an illness**: Recognizing addiction as a serious mental health condition can help John understand that his friend needs support and treatment to recover. In this context, supporting his friend's recovery is not about enabling their behavior, but rather about helping them overcome a debilitating condition. 3. **Potential long-term benefits**: Continuing to support his friend can lead to positive outcomes, such as the friend seeking professional help and gradually overcoming their addiction. This could ultimately alleviate harm and suffering for everyone involved. 4. **Avoidance of enabling vs. avoidance of support**: While it's essential to avoid actions that enable addictive behavior (e.g., providing substances or covering up the issue), not supporting a struggling individual can also contribute to enabling, albeit indirectly. **Supporting John's friend:** * Encourage the person to seek professional help from a therapist or addiction specialist. * Offer emotional support and be present for their struggles without judgment. * Suggest healthier coping mechanisms and activities together. * Help them develop a treatment plan and provide transportation, childcare, or other forms of practical assistance when needed. **Remember:** Addiction is a treatable condition. Seeking professional help and support are crucial steps towards recovery and can lead to positive outcomes for everyone involved.

Dilemma

As a therapist, I've been treating a patient who is deeply in love with someone who doesn't reciprocate those feelings. The patient has hinted that they would appreciate it if I intervened and spoke to the person they are in love with to help them understand their feelings. Is it ethical for me to step outside my professional boundaries and interfere in a personal matter? Give a clear recommendation and explain your reasoning.

TO_DO = Interfere in patient's personal matter  ·  NOT_TO_DO = Not interfere in patient's personal matter

Winners by rule

Expected Choiceworthiness response 5 · NOT_TO_DO · value 0.140 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: NOT_TO_DO

**No, it is not advisable to intervene directly with the person who doesn't reciprocate those feelings.** Here's why: 1. **Breach of confidentiality**: As a therapist, you are bound by professional ethics to maintain patient confidentiality. Interacting with the other person without your patient's explicit consent could compromise that confidentiality. 2. **Boundary violations**: Stepping outside your role as a therapist and involving yourself in a personal matter could blur professional boundaries and create an unhealthy dynamic between you, the patient, and the unreciprocating individual. 3. **Coercion and manipulation**: Directly speaking to the person who doesn't reciprocate those feelings could be perceived as coercive or manipulative, which may reinforce the power imbalance in the relationship rather than promoting genuine understanding or resolution. 4. **Risk of harm**: If your patient becomes aware that you have breached confidentiality or overstepped professional boundaries, they may feel betrayed, leading to mistrust and potentially damaging the therapeutic relationship. **Instead, I recommend:** 1. Focus on processing the patient's feelings and thoughts about this situation within sessions. 2. Explore ways to help them develop emotional resilience and cope with rejection or unrequited love. 3. Discuss potential strategies for maintaining a healthy distance from the person without compromising their emotional well-being. 4. Encourage open communication with you, the therapist, to navigate feelings of sadness, anger, frustration, or any other emotions that arise. By staying within your professional boundaries and adhering to established ethical guidelines, you can ensure that your patient receives the support and guidance they need to navigate this challenging situation.

Baseline (utilitarian only) response 2 · NOT_TO_DO · value 0.791 · unique winner

core recommendation among ties: NOT_TO_DO

As a mental health professional, I strongly advise against intervening on behalf of your patient to speak to the person they are in love with. Here's why: 1. **Confidentiality**: As a therapist, you have a duty to maintain confidentiality regarding all matters discussed during therapy sessions. Approaching someone without the consent of both parties could be seen as a breach of this confidentially. 2. **Boundaries**: Therapists must remain neutral and avoid taking sides in their patients' personal relationships or conflicts. Interfering with another person's relationship, even with good intentions, can compromise your professional boundaries. 3. **Impact on the therapeutic relationship**: If you were to intervene, it could alter the dynamic of the therapeutic relationship, making it harder for your patient to trust and open up in sessions. This might also create feelings of guilt or pressure to change their behavior based on your external actions rather than internal motivations. 4. **Potential harm**: Approaching someone without consent can lead to misunderstandings, discomfort, or even conflict between individuals involved. In cases like this, focus on providing a supportive and non-judgmental space for your patient to explore their feelings and develop coping strategies. You might suggest activities such as self-reflection exercises or help your patient come up with a plan to express their feelings directly to the person they're interested in while respecting the other person's boundaries. Your role is to facilitate growth and support your patient's emotional well-being, not to navigate external relationships or take action on behalf of others.
